# Recommended and audited references for MDT-GEN

Prepared 24 October 2026 after reviewing the current project manuscript, `docs/formula_references.md`, and the implementation areas for maternal prediction, CTGAN, Neural ODEs, fetal segmentation/biometry, and explainability. These sources are selected to match the project as implemented. A citation supports background or a method; it does not validate the project’s own model results.

## Priority references for clinical context and pregnancy outcomes

2. International Association of Diabetes and Pregnancy Study Groups Consensus Panel. International association of diabetes and pregnancy study groups recommendations on the diagnosis and classification of hyperglycemia in pregnancy. *Diabetes Care*. 2010;33(3):676–682. https://doi.org/10.2337/dc09-1848. — Diagnostic thresholds; state which diagnostic standard the project uses.
3. National Institute for Health and Care Excellence. *Diabetes in pregnancy: management from preconception to the postnatal period*. NICE guideline NG3. 2015, updated 2020. https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng3. — UK clinical pathway and screening/management context.
4. American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee. Management of diabetes in pregnancy: Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026. *Diabetes Care*. 2026;49(Suppl 1):S321 onward. https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S321/163918/15-Management-of-Diabetes-in-Pregnancy-Standards. — Current pregnancy diabetes care context.
5. HAPO Study Cooperative Research Group. Hyperglycemia and adverse pregnancy outcomes. *New England Journal of Medicine*. 2008;358:1991–2002. https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0707943. — Foundational evidence linking maternal glycemia and pregnancy outcomes.
6. Farrar D, Simmonds M, Bryant M, et al. Hyperglycaemia and risk of adverse perinatal outcomes: systematic review and meta-analysis. *BMJ*. 2016;354:i4694. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.i4694. — Evidence for associations; avoid implying causality beyond the review.

8. Zaky H, Fthenou E, Srour L, et al. ## Project-specific citation and claim audit

- This set gives more than 25 real, traceable references and maps them to the project’s current components. The most directly relevant are 8–9, 11–21, 25–29, and 31–39.
- The pasted reference list’s [1], [3], [5], [7], [9], and [10] contain DOI or bibliographic mismatches. Corrections for the works that are real are listed above.
- The manuscript’s reference [25] (“Maternal-fetal digital twins: integrating multi-omics and imaging informatics for perinatal precision medicine,” DOI `10.1038/s41746-025-01452-1`) is not that paper: the DOI belongs to *A phenotype-based AI pipeline outperforms human experts in differentially diagnosing rare diseases using EHRs*. Do not cite it as maternal-fetal digital-twin work.
- The manuscript’s reference [24] uses a DOI that does not match the IEEE digital-twin glucose paper. Use the corrected DOI above if that paper is retained.
- The manuscript’s reference [42] gives authors/title inconsistent with its DOI. Verify the intended clinical decision-support review and replace it with a correctly retrieved record, or use reference 40 for general clinical-AI translation context.
- The manuscript cites a Figshare identifier as an accessible maternal cohort. Verify that the DOI resolves to the described dataset and that its license/permissions cover this use before retaining the data-availability statement.
- ACOUSLIC-AI dataset v1.1 does provide fetal-abdomen masks, frame-level AC labels where measurable, and sweep-level reference AC. Its dataset description reports 744 × 562 frames and spacing of 0.2833 mm/pixel (commonly rounded to 0.28). The project also contains a dedicated AC inference path that restores the predicted mask to original frame resolution before ellipse fitting and applies the spacing. The older formula note describing only organ segmentation is therefore incomplete/stale relative to this AC-specific pipeline. The dataset and implementation make the AC approach appropriate to cite; report the exact dataset version, calibration, patient-level split, and held-out metrics used for the reported accuracy. The median-only AC reference described in the formula note cannot by itself support percentile claims; percentiles need a gestational-age-specific distribution.
- Clinical prediction caveat: outcome values such as delivery weight, birth outcome, intrapartum measurements, or later pregnancy labs must not leak into early-pregnancy predictors. Report the target definition, time point, split unit, external validation, calibration, uncertainty, and cohort provenance. TRIPOD+AI and PROBAST are relevant reporting/appraisal guides, not endorsements of any model result.
